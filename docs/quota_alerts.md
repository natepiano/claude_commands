# Quota alerts: the receiver's protocol

natedev's agent-sessions timer checks Claude and Codex weekly usage every 2 minutes (`~/.claude/scripts/whoami/agent_notes.py`, which writes `~/rust/hanadocs/agents/*.md`) and messages `always` and each showrunner in `~/.claude/config/showrunners.json` by session name. Claude readings cover `~/.claude` and every named account directory that passes `accounts.check`; spare accounts get fresh notes and readings too. The configured numbered groups serve showrunners and unit directors; the note for the login in `~/.claude` stays active too. Without account configuration or named directories, the existing single active Claude note and notice text stay the same. `/showrunner:produce` adds each showrunner. The timer also messages the user directly once when an account first enters a low-quota episode, and when a Codex refusal moves assignments to Claude. A failed low-quota message is retried by the next two-minute check, even after `/quota_ack`; a failed refusal message is retried by the next refusal. Successful low-quota messages do not repeat within the episode, and recovery sends no direct user message. Every new move to Claude sends a session notice and a direct user message, including after credits restored an earlier move or the refusing account became selected. A failed move is reported once per distinct failure text. A refusal that changes nothing repeats only until its direct message succeeds; an unknown account is reported once per home until a known account is reported there. Mechanics: the docstring of `quota_alert.py` beside it.
Account notes use numbered filenames (`claude1.md`, `codex2.md`) and stable labels (`claude1`, `codex2`). The updater writes `role` and `sessions`, and a `## Live sessions` table. An account is in use when it holds a role or a live session; the old `state` field is removed.

Notices arrive through `send.py` (/message) from the relay `quota_alert`, which cannot be answered; a session not running when one is sent has it queued. Tell them apart by the first line:

| First line | Means | Receiver does |
|---|---|---|
| `Quota alert:` | An account in use is at or under `threshold_percent`. Repeats every `repeat_minutes` until acknowledged; the user also receives the one direct message described above. | Claude: sessions on the named account start no new Claude work. A showrunner relays a unit-account notice to its units on that account. Codex: keep delegating, because the account may keep working on its credits and nothing is switched; if Codex refuses work for quota, act on that notice at once. Treat repeats as one item. |
| `Quota alert:` saying every function on Codex was moved to Claude | Codex refused work for quota, the registry was switched to Claude, and the user was messaged directly for this move. | Keep delegating, now on Claude; re-run Codex work that was in flight. |
| `5-hour limit:` | Claude or Codex has 20% or less of its 5-hour window left (`five_hour.py`). Sent once per low spell. | Tell the user in one line; change nothing else. They are sent it directly unless they type in a terminal within 15 minutes (`escalate.py`). |
| `Quota alert acknowledged:` | The user silenced it, in the session named. | Drop held copies. Paused work stays paused. |
| `Quota restored:` | The named account is back above the threshold: a used limit reset, a new window, or its own window reset. Codex assignments can return on allowance or credits, independent of low-quota episodes. | Resume work paused for that account and drop its held alerts. Other accounts keep their own episodes. |

## Commands

- `/quota_ack [note]` — the user's alone (`disable-model-invocation`). Naming a note silences only that account; omitting it acknowledges every open episode. Never run it or `quota_alert.py ack` yourself, and never treat an alert as acknowledged.
- `/quota_refresh` — run it when the user says they reset usage. It refreshes the notes now and sends `Quota restored:` to every configured session but yours.

Every user act is echoed to every configured session except the one it happened in. What to do about the quota itself — redeem a limit reset, switch accounts — is the user's call. A Codex alert's second line gives the account's credit balance as Codex reports it, or says none is reported. Only a Codex alert states a limit-reset count, the one its refresh read; nothing reads a Claude count live, so a Claude alert states none and offers only another login, and each refresh sets a Claude note's `limit_reset_count` and `limit_reset` to null. Above zero, reaching the low threshold switches nothing. At weekly zero, an account with `use_credits: off` moves workers as described below; with credits on, a provider refusal still decides the move. The launcher reports the serving account, refusal time and any window named in the refusal text through `agent_notes.py blocked --window weekly|five_hour|unknown --refused-at <aware ISO time>`. Otherwise a reading taken after the refusal identifies the window only when exactly one window is at zero. The refusal is recorded and overrides saved positive percentages.

A known 5-hour refusal waits when its known reset is in the future and less than 20 minutes away. One notice says `workers wait for <label>'s 5-hour reset at <time>`. Exactly 20 minutes, a passed reset, or an unknown window or reset causes a move instead. The picker excludes the refusing home and chooses the Codex account with room whose weekly reset is soonest, credits included. The `workers` role changes through the same path as `swap workers`; live turns finish and each worker moves at its next idle boundary. The notice says `workers moved from <old> to <new>: <old> refused (<window> limit until <reset>)`. A refused turn owes a continuation: once its new home is available, or its wait ends, it starts `Continue where you stopped.` before held messages in order.

When no other Codex account has room, the existing Claude fallback changes assignments and its notice names the earliest reset. A failed move to the picked home also opens that fallback; the notice names the failed move and its cause. Owed assignments return when the selected Codex account can work and its refusal has resolved; assignments changed by the user meanwhile stay as chosen. A refusal from an outgoing account changes no role or assignments. A refusal creates no weekly episode. The natedev session shows fallback changes on the user's next prompt.

Claude notes are active for the login in `~/.claude` and every login local live sessions use. Notices follow those accounts even when showrunners and units run on several logins. A low-account notice says `<label> is low; sessions on it move to <picked label> when refused`, or that no other account has room until the earliest reset. The destination comes from fresh usage and recorded refusals, with the soonest weekly reset first and no percentage floor. Alerts offer no unit-role swap command; `claude-unit` starts on the picked account. Codex workers retain their selected home until a provider refusal or the credits-off weekly-zero policy chooses a transition.

Each refresh freezes the login in `~/.claude` and local live sessions’ logins and config directories once for note activity and notices. Moving sessions off a low Claude account preserves its episode and acknowledgement. Returning to it stays quiet if it was acknowledged; only its own quota recovery or weekly reset closes the episode. Missing or unknown readings preserve it too. A recovered account sends a notice for that account alone. New sessions pick independently of their role; resumes retain their recorded account unless explicitly moved.

Each Codex pass uses one worker account selection, its known or unknown sign-in, and the live reading and observation time of every checked home. The updater builds this value before writing notes and passes it to weekly alerts, the 5-hour watcher and refresh. Spare Codex accounts raise no low-quota alert. Making an account spare stops delivery and keeps its weekly episode and acknowledgement; selecting a still-low acknowledged account again stays quiet until its own quota recovers or resets. Unknown identity preserves episodes and pending holds.

A Codex alert names its account and says `workers move to <picked> when Codex refuses work`, or `no other Codex account has room until <earliest reset>`. Above zero it uses the picker for advice; at zero the account’s credits setting decides whether workers stay or move.

Codex 5-hour holds and relay keys belong to a home's signed-in account. A known worker account selection cancels holds for outgoing accounts and the old tool-wide hold and their queued relays without a recovery message, including when the selected account has no usage window. The relay checks ownership again before sending, so a swap before the next updater run still cancels outgoing news. Unknown identity leaves Codex holds alone. Holds for other tools continue independently.

A proven turn refusal carries the serving server's account, including after the server is retired or its record is removed. Unknown refusing identity is reported as unknown and opens no fallback. A release failure or failed reviewer preparation command is not a provider refusal. Fallback state distinguishes no fallback, an account-owned fallback and a legacy fallback whose owner is unknown.

`~/.local/state/accounts/moves.jsonl` records each transition under an append lock. Entries share the time, tool, subject and stable move id. A `workers` role write records `requested`, not confirmation; `codex_mesh.py` writes one `confirmed` entry per worker when it resumes on the destination home. Failures, waits and their end, no room and room again are separate entries. Readers skip malformed lines, including when checking for duplicate confirmations. A request omits `reset` when unknown; waits always carry a known reset. Refusal state lives beside it in `refusals.json`. Saving usage is best effort: a store failure is logged and does not prevent alerts; advice text only reads the saved store.

`~/.claude/scripts/lib/py ~/.claude/scripts/accounts/accounts.py burn --since <ISO> [--until <ISO>]` reports points spent per observed day for each account and the roles it served. It reads `~/.local/state/agent-notes/readings.jsonl` and `~/.local/state/accounts/roles.jsonl`; before 2026-10-13 00:00 UTC, the login in `~/.claude`'s note label identifies historical showrunner usage. From that cutover onward, readings are labeled by account alone, with no role attribution. Historical unit directory selections resolve to the note label of that directory's login before matching readings. An unreadable historical identity refuses the report; it never picks a note label without one. Role changes split the report, including when unit directors share the account in `~/.claude`. Decreases between paired readings measure spend; an increase in remaining allowance starts a new span, so a weekly refill never becomes negative spend. Window edges and role changes within an observation interval divide its spend in proportion to elapsed time. Unobserved time and the interval across a refill contribute neither spend nor observed days. One reading alone gives no rate. `--until` defaults to now; an ISO timestamp without an offset is interpreted in the user's zone, set in `~/.claude/config/user_zone.conf`. The command reads local data and launches nothing.

## Codex weekly usage at zero

Each `codexN.md` account note accepts `use_credits: on|off`. Missing or invalid values mean `on`; invalid values produce one log line per alert pass. The setting follows the login, including a numbered account held in `~/.codex`. The updater preserves it.

At 0% weekly with a future refill, the workers' selected account opens one zero episode. The same catalog text goes once to the phone and configured recipients plus running showrunners. A failed phone delivery retries every pass; sessions receive no second copy. This replaces that account's low-threshold episode silently: no repeat, acknowledgement request or restored message at zero. Above zero, the existing low alert still applies. A refill, fresh positive reading or selection of another account closes the zero episode silently.

With credits on, workers stay on paid credits. The notice names the balance, refill and destination when credits run out. With credits off, workers prefer accounts with included usage in both windows, choosing the soonest weekly refill among them. Only when none has weekly room may they move to an account using its own paid credits; the notice states that explicitly. Live turns finish first. This request records `cause: credits off`, distinct from a provider refusal. If no account has room or the move fails, that pass moves Codex assignments to Claude through the existing fallback and names the earliest refill and any failure. The fallback stays in place until that refill or a selected account recovers; switching the exhausted account back to credits on does not undo it early. A candidate with credits off cannot buy room with its credit balance.

While credits are on, an hourly forecast goes to those recipients; only the first hourly forecast also goes to the phone, with phone-only retries on failure. It uses decreases in numeric balances from `agent-notes/readings.jsonl`, skips purchases and divides usage by the observed interval. Less than 30 minutes of observation or a newest reading older than 10 minutes makes usage unknown. Whole-credit rounding may report zero use; a missing observation never means zero use. Unlimited and unknown balances are stated explicitly.

The paid-credits opening notice gives the exact account note and an action: set `use_credits: off` to request a move within a few minutes. An episode on credits rereads the setting on every alert pass. The first on-to-off change requests the credits-off move once, stops forecasts and repeats no opening alert; changing back to on changes nothing in that episode. The opening phone notice includes a forecast when observations support one, and states unknown usage or an unlimited balance explicitly.
