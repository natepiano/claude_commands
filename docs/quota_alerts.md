# Quota alerts: the receiver's protocol

natedev's two-minute notes timer checks Claude and Codex weekly readings. Account notes have numbered labels, role lists, live-session counts and a `## Live sessions` table; an account is in use when it has a role or a live session.

## Claude: what happens next

`/next_moves` manages an ordered one-shot log on natedev. Say “when units reach 1% switch to claude2”, “what's set up” or “drop the units one”. A hold may be requested in minutes. The Mac reads and edits the same log through natedev. See `docs/accounts.md` for the CLI, hold semantics and chains.

At 2% weekly or below, an in-use Claude account with any uncovered group sends one phone reminder per episode. Its title is “Claude work on claude3 may stop”, its body is “claude3 has 2% of its weekly usage left, and no move is set up for the units or showrunners on it. When it runs out, they move to a Claude account with room, or stop if none has any.” and its action is “In the natedev session, type /next_moves and the move you want, for example: when units reach 1% switch to claude2.” Only entries targeting another account and backed by readable role history count as coverage; entries covering every group on the account suppress it; accounts used only by ungrouped sessions send nothing. A failed delivery retries at the next check; success never repeats within the episode. Missing readings preserve the episode; known recovery or reset closes it silently.

Claude sends no weekly session alert, 30-minute repeat, showrunner relay, `Quota restored:` notice or instruction to start no new work. `/quota_ack` and its acknowledgement state are removed. `/quota_refresh` still refreshes readings when the user reports a reset; routing restoration is recorded in the moves journal without a session notice. A scheduled move consumes its entry when its group selection is recorded, keeps the usual safe shutdown/showrunner notices, and sends no phone alert.

## Codex notices

Workers use the same one-shot log: “when workers reach 1% switch to codex2” or “when workers reach 1% switch to claude”. Workers entries have no minute hold. A move to Claude holds there until the source account's weekly reset, even if Codex has room earlier. Entries run once in log order and leave the log after a recorded move; refused swaps keep them.

Above 0% and at or below 2%, Codex sends one phone reminder per episode when there is no workers entry targeting another account and no scheduled Claude hold is open. At 0%, the default move is handled in the same pass and no reminder is sent. A delivered reminder stays delivered across zero and subsequent readings until the weekly reset. An unreadable move journal makes coverage empty. The reminder names the default destination picked now, excluding the low account, and mentions paid credits only when that account allows them. It offers a concrete `/next_moves` command to choose that destination explicitly; leaving the default needs no action. The title names paid credit use when credits are allowed, or the destination when they are not.

A quota refusal that stops a worker command sends one message to that worker's unit director, naming the exact command and saying to rerun it on Claude. The launcher supplies `--stopped <session id> <worker> <command>` only when the command exits, with its director found from the run-active marker, else the launcher ancestry. Every exited refusal sends this message, including while fallback routing is already open, and clears the stopped worker's owed continuation. Unknown directors receive no message. A waiting follow receives none and keeps its continuation.

Zero, credits forecasts, Codex-to-Codex moves and unknown-account refusals write log lines without session delivery. Phone pushes, move journal rows and the natedev prompt notice remain. Restoration is recorded only in `moves.jsonl` as `routed back` or `routing not restored`; `/quota_refresh` tells nobody. Showrunners keep no held quota-alert list. The legacy configuration `repeat_minutes` is unused; `always` supplies recipients only for Claude five-hour and Claude no-room notices, alongside running showrunners.

The Codex `5-hour limit:` notice from `five_hour.py` writes one log line per low spell, with its back-above line also logged. The Claude five-hour notice is unchanged: tell the user in one line and change nothing else. Escalation goes to the phone unless the user types in a terminal within 15 minutes.

A Codex notice gives the reported credits and limit-reset count. Without a next-move entry, low quota above zero switches nothing. At weekly zero, `use_credits: off` requests a move; with credits on, a provider refusal still decides the move. The launcher reports the serving account, refusal time and known window through `agent_notes.py blocked --window weekly|five_hour|unknown --refused-at <aware ISO time>`. A later reading identifies the window only when exactly one window is at zero. Recorded refusal overrides positive saved percentages.

A known 5-hour refusal waits when its known reset is in the future and less than 20 minutes away. One log line says `workers wait for <label>'s 5-hour reset at <time>`. Exactly 20 minutes, a passed reset, or an unknown window or reset causes a move instead. The picker excludes the refusing home and chooses the Codex account with room whose weekly reset is soonest, credits included. The `workers` role changes through the same path as `swap workers`; live turns finish and each worker moves at its next idle boundary. The log says `workers moved from <old> to <new>: <old> refused (<window> limit until <reset>)`. A refused turn in a waiting follow owes a continuation: once its new home is available, or its wait ends, it starts `Continue where you stopped.` before held messages in order. An exited command instead leaves its rerun to the director.

When no other Codex account has room, the existing Claude fallback changes assignments and its notice names the earliest reset. A failed move to the picked home also opens that fallback; the notice names the failed move and its cause. Owed assignments return when the selected Codex account can work and its refusal has resolved; assignments changed by the user meanwhile stay as chosen. A refusal from an outgoing account changes no role or assignments. A refusal creates no weekly episode. The natedev session shows fallback changes on the user's next prompt.

Each Codex pass fixes one worker selection, identity and checked home's live reading with its observation time. Spare accounts raise no low alert; making one spare preserves its episode until its own quota recovers or resets. Unknown identity preserves episodes and pending holds.

Codex move advice names its account and says `workers move to <picked> when Codex refuses work`, or `no other Codex account has room until <earliest reset>`. Above zero it uses the picker for advice; at zero the account’s credits setting decides whether workers stay or move.

Codex five-hour markers belong to a home's signed-in account and suppress repeated log lines during a low spell. Cleanup still retires queued Codex relays and escalation holds from older versions when the account changes, including when the selected account has no usage window. Unknown identity leaves that state alone. Claude notices continue independently.

A proven turn refusal carries the serving server's account, including after the server is retired or its record is removed. Unknown refusing identity is reported as unknown and opens no fallback. A release failure or failed reviewer preparation command is not a provider refusal. Fallback state distinguishes no fallback, an account-owned fallback and a legacy fallback whose owner is unknown.

`~/.local/state/accounts/moves.jsonl` records each transition under an append lock. Entries share the time, tool, subject and stable move id. A `workers` role write records `requested`, not confirmation; `codex_mesh.py` writes one `confirmed` entry per worker when it resumes on the destination home. Failures, waits and their end, no room and room again are separate entries. Readers skip malformed lines, including when checking for duplicate confirmations. A request omits `reset` when unknown; waits always carry a known reset. Refusal state lives beside it in `refusals.json`. Saving usage is best effort: a store failure is logged and does not prevent alerts; advice text only reads the saved store.

`~/.claude/scripts/lib/py ~/.claude/scripts/accounts/accounts.py burn --since <ISO> [--until <ISO>]` reports points spent per observed day for each account and the roles it served. It reads `~/.local/state/agent-notes/readings.jsonl` and `~/.local/state/accounts/roles.jsonl`; before 2026-10-13 00:00 UTC, the login in `~/.claude`'s note label identifies historical showrunner usage. From that cutover onward, readings are labeled by account alone, with no role attribution. Historical unit directory selections resolve to the note label of that directory's login before matching readings. An unreadable historical identity refuses the report; it never picks a note label without one. Role changes split the report, including when unit directors share the account in `~/.claude`. Decreases between paired readings measure spend; an increase in remaining allowance starts a new span, so a weekly refill never becomes negative spend. Window edges and role changes within an observation interval divide its spend in proportion to elapsed time. Unobserved time and the interval across a refill contribute neither spend nor observed days. One reading alone gives no rate. `--until` defaults to now; an ISO timestamp without an offset is interpreted in the user's zone, set in `~/.claude/config/user_zone.conf`. The command reads local data and launches nothing.

## Codex weekly usage at zero

Each `codexN.md` account note accepts `use_credits: on|off`. Missing or invalid values mean `on`; invalid values produce one log line per alert pass. The setting follows the login, including a numbered account held in `~/.codex`. The updater preserves it.

At 0% weekly with a future refill, the workers' selected account opens one zero episode. The catalog text is logged once and sent to the phone. A failed phone delivery retries every pass; sessions receive no copy. The separate low reminder remains one phone push per episode; it has no session alert or repeat. A scheduled move to Claude holds until its source reset and prevents the zero policy from undoing the move. A refill, fresh positive reading or selection of another account closes the zero episode silently.

With credits on, workers stay on paid credits. The notice names the balance, refill and destination when credits run out. With credits off, workers prefer accounts with included usage in both windows, choosing the soonest weekly refill among them. Only when none has weekly room may they move to an account using its own paid credits; the notice states that explicitly. Live turns finish first. This request records `cause: credits off`, distinct from a provider refusal. If no account has room or the move fails, that pass moves Codex assignments to Claude through the existing fallback and names the earliest refill and any failure. The fallback stays in place until that refill or a selected account recovers; switching the exhausted account back to credits on does not undo it early. A candidate with credits off cannot buy room with its credit balance.

While credits are on, an hourly forecast is logged; only the first hourly forecast also goes to the phone, with phone-only retries on failure. It uses decreases in numeric balances from `agent-notes/readings.jsonl`, skips purchases and divides usage by the observed interval. Less than 30 minutes of observation or a newest reading older than 10 minutes makes usage unknown. Whole-credit rounding may report zero use; a missing observation never means zero use. Unlimited and unknown balances are stated explicitly.

The paid-credits opening notice gives the exact account note and an action: set `use_credits: off` to request a move within a few minutes. An episode on credits rereads the setting on every alert pass. The first on-to-off change requests the credits-off move once, stops forecasts and repeats no opening alert; changing back to on changes nothing in that episode. The opening phone notice includes a forecast when observations support one, and states unknown usage or an unlimited balance explicitly.


Replay checks defer entries when their journal cannot be read or decoded. An undecodable final line with no trailing newline is skipped with a log naming the file; an undecodable middle line still blocks. Rows are appended as complete encoded lines. See `docs/accounts.md` for the engine guards and install order.
