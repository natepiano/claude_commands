### Phase 3 — /build:queue reads a lock-free snapshot and answers in under a second every time  · status: done

#### As-built

- `memory_admit.gather_queue_inputs(directory, processes, now, memory={}, *, readings=READING_NOT_TAKEN) -> QueueInputs` does every file, `/proc` and environment read; the pure `queue_read_from(inputs) -> QueueRead` reads none. `QueueInputs` holds `ordered`, `running`, `reservations`, `host`, `pressure`, raw `recovery: RecoverySpacing`, `limits: AdmissionLimits` (resolved once by `resolve_admission_limits`, the one resolver that `decide` also calls), `override` and `now`. `read_queue_pass`, the locked read and the snapshot run this one decision path.
- `exclusive_queue(directory)` yields `QueueSnapshotReadings` and, in its finally block, publishes `admission/queue.json` (`{"v": 1, "written_at", "inputs"}`) through `write_queue_snapshot` (`queue.json.tmp` + `os.replace`, no fsync). Every exclusive section, `admit_head` included, publishes this way, also on an exception path; a failed write never changes the section's result. A bump takes no lock and writes no snapshot.
- `queue_read() -> QueueRead | QueueBusy` uses the snapshot when `0 <= age <= SNAPSHOT_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` (10 s). Else it calls `locked_queue_read(timeout=QUEUE_READ_LOCK_SECONDS)` (0.5 s). On timeout, a parsed but old snapshot gives `QueueRead.freshness = StaleReading(written_at)` (the view header time prints in the warning tone); a missing or bad file gives `QueueBusy`. `build_queue.py` (view, card, `--json`) and `build_why.py` print `queue busy; try again` on `QueueBusy` and exit 0.

### Phase 4 — One decision pass decides for every waiting step, and the check and the queue read both call it  · status: done

#### As-built

- `admission_pass(inputs: QueueInputs, now: float) -> PassResult` in `memory_admit.py` is the one decision for every waiting row. It is pure: no file, `/proc`, environment or clock read, and no write. It calls `admission_walk` once for each input scenario and reads every row's decision from that one walk. A running step that ends is its own scenario: `inputs_after_steps_end(inputs, ended)` makes the changed copy, and one walk runs on it. `queue_decision` and `reservation_blocker` are gone.
- `PassResult` holds `rows: tuple[PassRow, ...]` in queue order, `head: HeadPriority | NoHeadPriority`, `totals: AdmissionDecision` (`decide(available, total, 0, memory)`), `recovery: RecoverySpacing`, `head_marks: tuple[HeadMark, ...]` and `override: OverrideKept | OverrideEnded`. `PassRow` holds `owner` (`<pid>-<start_time>`, from `waiter_owner`), `decision`, `reason`, `blocker: BlockingIndex | NoSingleBlocker | BlockerNotRequested` and `admission: ProposedAdmission | NotAdmitted`. `ProposedAdmission` holds `rank`, `recovery` and `override`. A `HeadMark(path, waiter)` sets `holds_younger` and `priority_since` on a head that has no `priority_since`. `OverrideEnded.action` is `"admitted"` or `"lapsed"`.
- Rules of the pass: each admitted row charges its need to the rows after it. In a recovery window (`RecoveryStarts`) at most one row admits; its proposal sets `next_start_at = now + RECOVERY_START_SECONDS`, and `PassResult.recovery` keeps the start due. `next_recovery(recovery, pressure, now)` is the one recovery change. `estimate_expired(waiter, now)` is the one expiry rule (estimate `HEAD_SECONDS` or more old): the row holds with `EstimateExpired(estimated_at)`, a `WaitReason`, and keeps its place and its room. A `ReadingUnavailable` host holds every row.
- Proposed, not committed. `check` builds `QueueInputs` with `gather_queue_inputs` from the readings it already took, adds its own waiter row, sets `blockers_for` to its own owner, calls the pass and commits only its own row. `commit_pass_effects` writes its proposal's `recovery` and `override`, or `PassResult.recovery` and `PassResult.override` when its row does not admit, before the reservation write. `check` writes the head marks and prints `memory admission: estimate expired while waiting for the lock; refresh on the next poll` when its own row expires. A launch with no ledger (no `admission/` folder) decides alone through `decide()`.
- `QueueInputs.blockers_for: frozenset[str]` names the owners that get a blocker; every other row has `BlockerNotRequested`. `blocking_index(owner, endings)` finds the one running step whose end admits the row. `blocker_identity(index, reservations, processes, proc) -> Blocker` resolves it at the process and sidecar boundary, for `check`, `blocker_snapshot` and `current_blocker`. The snapshot codec (`snapshot_value`, `parse_snapshot_value`) writes a `frozenset` as a sorted list.

3. **What the service holds.** One single-threaded loop on one `select.epoll`, with no threads and no locks. In memory:
   - waiters: the connection, the owner (pid, start time), the episode id (Spec 5), the profile, its `fresh_test` flag and need, `estimated_at`, `since`, today's order fields that `admission_pass` reads (`bump_order`, `holds_younger`, `priority_since`), and the identity (session id, name, step, folder). Each waiter's key path in `QueueInputs.ordered` is `<state dir>/<owner>`, with `<owner>` = `waiter_owner(row)` (`<pid>-<start_time>`); no file is written at it. `HeadMark.path` and `head_override_matches` match on it.

The service keys the place (its `since` and order fields) by owner and first `since`, from the step's first `admit` until its owner exits. A later `admit` from the same owner with the same first `since` (the token path's second acquire) is a new episode that takes that same place. There is one wait rule: a short wait lets other steps pass, and a long wait blocks until the step has what it needs. That rule is `admission_pass`'s; the service adds none.
   - No time at head (user, 2026-10-31). The waiter record has no `head_since`, no `head_state` and no other field for the time a step has been first.
   - No bump end time (user, 2026-10-31). The protocol has no bump request, and the waiter record has no bump field with an end time. When resources Phase 26 brings the user's bump, it holds until the user bumps something else.
   - Showrunner note (2026-10-31): resources Phase 26 can change these waiter fields; Phase 10 carries that change into the protocol and the record.
   - reservations: a `Reservation` row for each (owner, need, `admitted_at`, width as `test_threads`, call id, shape (resources Phase 24), sidecar and `reservation_id`), with the episode that admitted it and the scope cgroup path;

   - the recovery spacing and the last host reading (meminfo, PSI, sccache anon);
   - the last pass: its `QueueInputs` and its `PassResult`;
   - the replay ledger (Spec 5): for each admitted episode its `admitted` reply, and for each mutation request id its reply.

   Persisted: one file, `<state dir>/state.json`, written through a temporary file and `os.replace` after each change to the reservations, the waiter places, the admit-head record, the recovery spacing or the replay ledger. Its records go through the same generic codec (`snapshot_value`, `parse_snapshot_value`). Waiters persist only their place (`since` and order fields) and their episode id, keyed by owner. 4. **Start, restart and owners.**

For each reservation it checks the owner (pid and start time, `process_start_time`) and the scope (`cgroup.procs`) with `reservation_lifetime` (`PopulatedStepScope | LiveOwnerProcess | EndedStep`). Live rows keep their charge; ended rows end as below. Then it sends `READY=1` to `$NOTIFY_SOCKET` when it is set.
   - Each loop turn sends `WATCHDOG=1` to `$NOTIFY_SOCKET` when it is set.
   - Ending a reservation: the service calls today's `finalize_reservation(stem, row, outcome, now, status)`. It writes its saved intent into a file at the stem path before the history append to `<state dir>/anon_peaks.jsonl`, and deletes that file and the `.max`, `.wave` and sidecar files at its end. The row's `reservation_id`, set at admission, makes a second run append no second history row. At start, a reservation whose stem file still exists is read with `read_reservation(stem)` and finishes from its saved intent. After `finalize_reservation` returns, the service drops the entry from `state.json`. Phase 10 moves the intent into `state.json` (`StateIntents`), and no stem file is written.
   - The restart rule (Decided block, 2026-10-30) is built here.
   - The service takes its listening socket from systemd (`LISTEN_FDS`). It binds `admission_socket_path()` itself only when `LISTEN_FDS` is absent (tests, a run by hand).
   - A waiter ends when its connection closes or its owner exits. A reservation ends on `release`, or when its owner has exited and its scope is empty; a reservation with no scope ends when its owner exits. The service watches each owner with `os.pidfd_open`, and each scope's `cgroup.events` for `populated 0` (registered on the epoll with `EPOLLPRI`: kernfs wakes pollers when the file changes; read the file again after each wake). Both are kernel events, so no polling.

5. **Protocol.** Newline-delimited JSON on a Unix stream socket. One request per connection. Every request carries `"v": 1`. A request with an unknown `v` gets `refused {"reason": "client version N, service supports 1..M"}`, and the client stops with that line.

   | Request | Reply |
   | --- | --- |
   | `admit` {episode, owner {pid, start_time}, argv, cwd, since, sidecar, identity {session_id, name, step, folder}, call_id, profile, fresh_test} | `wait` {reason, position, head, text} lines on each change, and at least every 60 s; then one `admitted` {reservation, need, source, max_path, wave_path} or `refused` {reason} |
   | `launch` {episode, owner, since, identity, profile} (BRP, no argv) | `wait` lines as for `admit`; then one `launch_admitted` {need, source} or `refused` {reason}. No reservation is made, so the reply has no reservation and no sidecar paths. |
   | `release` {request_id, reservation, status} | `ok` |
   | `width` {request_id, reservation, width} | `ok` |
   | `view`, `explain`, `mine` {session_id} | One JSON answer from the last pass |
   | `admit_head` {request_id, why, actor} | `ok` or `refused` |

   - Two admission outcomes, one for each kind of call. `admitted` answers only an `admit`: it always carries the reservation and its `.max` and `.wave` paths, which the service builds from the stem (Spec 1). `launch_admitted` answers only a `launch`. The client's parser refuses an `admitted` reply to a `launch` and a `launch_admitted` reply to an `admit`.
   - Launch checks stay as Phase 4 left them. A launch is a BRP app start with no argv and no reservation. When the service holds no waiter and no reservation (its form of today's absent `admission/` folder) and the pressure reading has no hold, the launch decides alone: `decide(available, total, need, sccache_memory(), pressure)`, with its own need and the compiler-service memory. It gets no waiter row, no head priority and no wait credit from its `since`, so an aged launch never gets the head's free-memory bypass. In every other case the launch waits in the pass as a waiter, and on admission the service commits that pass's recovery and override effects and makes no reservation.
   - Admission episodes. The client makes one `episode` id (a random UUID) when one `admit` or `launch` call starts, and sends the same id on each reconnect of that call, `EstimateExpired` resends included. A new acquire by the same owner is a new call, so it has a new id: `verify.sh`'s token path (`buildlog_wait_for_memory`: it admits, releases, waits for a cargo token, then waits again with its first `since`) makes two episodes for one shell, and the second takes the first one's place (Spec 3). The service keys reservations and the ledger by episode, and the place by owner and first `since`.
   - Replay. The service writes `state.json` with the new reservation and its ledger entry before it sends `admitted`; it records a `launch_admitted` reply in the ledger the same way. An `admit` whose episode already has a reservation gets that same `admitted` reply again: no second charge and no second queue place. Each mutation (`release`, `width`, `admit_head`) carries a `request_id` (a random UUID the client makes once and resends on reconnect); the service writes its change and its reply to the ledger in one `state.json` write before it replies, and a repeated `request_id` gets the recorded reply with no second change.
   - `wait.reason` is the `WaitReason` name. `wait.text` is the phrase `queue_view.reason_phrase` gives it.
   - On a `wait` with reason `EstimateExpired` (Phase 4), the client calls its `refresh` again, closes, and sends a new `admit` with the same episode and its first `since`. The service keeps the waiter's place for that episode, so the refresh moves no step.
   - The service writes the build log rows that the lock holder writes today, from the same functions: `anon_peaks.jsonl` at release through `finalize_reservation`, and the admit-head rows through `head_override_event` (the function `end_head_override` calls; the service has no `admit-head.json` file to delete). It does not call `commit_pass_effects`, which writes the recovery and admit-head files.
   - The socket file is mode 0600, so only the user's processes connect. The service records `actor`. The client CLI's `admit-head` calls `require_showrunner` and sends its result as `actor` (design §4).
   - An `admit_head` takes the first waiter in `waiter_order` and builds its `HeadAdmissionOverride` with today's fields: `waiter` (the key path's name), `pid`, `start_time`, `unit` (`waiter_unit_name`), `step`, `need`, `free` and `reserve` (`decide(available, total, need, []).reserve` on the last host reading), `why` and `set_by` (the `actor`). It writes the `set` row with `head_override_event`. With no waiter it replies `refused {"reason": "nothing waits; no head to admit"}`.
6. **When a pass runs.** On every event: an `admit`, a `launch`, a close, a `release`, an owner exit, an empty scope, a `width` or an `admit_head`. Also once a second while any waiter waits, because memory freed outside builds shows only in `/proc/meminfo`. A pass reads meminfo, PSI, sccache's `memory.stat` and each running scope's `memory.current` and `memory.swap.current` through the readers in `memory_admit.py` (`read_meminfo`, `read_pressure`, `sccache_memory`, `reservation_memory`). It reads the `/proc` tree only for reservations with no scope. It builds `QueueInputs` (Spec 1) and calls `admission_pass(inputs, now)` (Phase 4), and acts on the result: it commits the proposed admissions in `ProposedAdmission.rank` order, keeps the `recovery` and `override` of each admission it commits (when it commits none, `PassResult.recovery` and `PassResult.override`) in its own state, writes the `admitted` or `lapsed` row with `head_override_event` for an `OverrideEnded`, applies each `HeadMark.waiter` to the waiter on its key path, and sends a `wait` line to each waiter whose reason or position changed. The service makes no admission decision of its own: `admission_pass` is the only decision code it calls, and the launch-alone case calls `decide` as Phase 4's `check` does.
7. **The client, `admission_client.py`.**
   - Python API: `admit(request: AdmitRequest, on_wait: Callable[[Wait], None], refresh: Callable[[], AdmitRequest]) -> Admitted | Refused`, `launch(request: LaunchRequest, on_wait: Callable[[Wait], None], refresh: Callable[[], LaunchRequest]) -> LaunchAdmitted | Refused`, `release(reservation: str, status: int)`, `width(reservation: str, width: int)`, `view() -> QueueRead`, `explain() -> QueueExplanation`, `mine(session_id: str)`, `admit_head(why: str, actor: str)`. The client keeps the first request's episode and `since` on every resend; `refresh` gives only a new estimate.
   - CLI: one subcommand for each query and mutation (`release`, `width`, `view`, `explain`, `mine`, `admit-head`). Phase 10 adds `admit`, `launch` and `down-alert`.
   - Queries are bounded. `view`, `explain` and `mine` wait for no admission: each connects and reads its one answer within `QUERY_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (0.5 s) in all, or raises `AdmissionUnavailable` with the reason (`no socket`, `connect refused`, `no answer in 0.5 s`). It does not try again. Their CLI prints `admission service unavailable: <reason>` on stderr and exits 2.
   - The outcomes below are for `admit`, `launch` and the mutations, which each resend with the same episode or `request_id`, so a retry is safe (Spec 5). Phase 10 replaces the shell's own rules in `memory_gate.sh` with them, and Phase 7's wait rules stay true through them; there is no closed list of `source` values. The client sorts each failure into exactly one of three outcomes, and each has its own rule:
     - Transport unavailable: the service is gone or silent (no socket, a refused connect, a closed connection, or no `wait` line for 120 s). The client never refuses for this and never falls back to the lock (design §5). A closed connection: it connects again at once, sends its first request again with its first `since` and its episode or `request_id`, and prints `admission service restarted; waiting again` once. No socket, or a refused connect: it tries again every 2 s and prints one line a minute. No `wait` line for 120 s: it prints `admission service not answering since HH:MM` (time in the user zone, through `user_zone.py`) and keeps waiting. The first call follows the same rule: a client that finds no service at its first connect keeps trying.
     - Checker failure while waiting: the client cannot get a valid answer for its step although it reached the service. That is, its `refresh` raises (on an `EstimateExpired` refresh), or a reply line fails `admission_protocol.py`'s parser. Phase 7's rule holds: after the first `wait` line, the client prints `memory admission check failed (<reason>); trying again, the step keeps its place`, resends with the same episode and first `since`, and refuses only after `BUILDLOG_MEM_CHECK_RETRY_S` (default 60 s, a value that is not a whole number gives 60) of failures with no valid answer between them. A valid `wait` line resets that count. An empty failure reason uses Phase 7's fallback reason (`checker returned an error`). A failure before the first `wait` line refuses at once, with no retry.
     - Explicit refusal: a `refused` reply. The client stops at once with its reason. It does not retry.
     - Launch deadline: a `launch` that has no `launch_admitted` within `BUILDLOG_MEM_WAIT_LIMIT_S` (default 900 s; 0 refuses at the first hold) refuses with `launch wait limit reached without admission`. The limit runs on all three paths: a transport wait and a checker retry never extend it. An `admit` has no such limit.
   - The client prints the outcome lines above on stderr and gives each `wait` message to `on_wait`. Today's wait line form comes in Phase 10.

A closed connection: it connects again at once, sends its first request again with its first `since` and its episode or `request_id`, and prints `admission service restarted; waiting again` once. No socket, or a refused connect: it tries again every 2 s and prints one line a minute. No `wait` line for 120 s: it prints `admission service not answering since HH:MM` (time in the user zone, through `user_zone.py`) and keeps waiting. The first call follows the same rule: a client that finds no service at its first connect keeps trying.
     - Checker failure while waiting: the client cannot get a valid answer for its step although it reached the service. That is, its `step_estimate` raises (at the start or on an `EstimateExpired` refresh), or a reply line fails `admission_protocol.py`'s parser. Phase 7's rule holds: after the first `wait` line, the client prints `memory admission check failed (<reason>); trying again, the step keeps its place`, resends with the same episode and first `since`, and refuses only after `BUILDLOG_MEM_CHECK_RETRY_S` (default 60 s, a value that is not a whole number gives 60) of failures with no valid answer between them. A valid `wait` line resets that count. An empty failure reason uses Phase 7's fallback reason. A failure before the first `wait` line refuses at once, with no retry.
     - Explicit refusal: a `refused` reply. The client stops at once with its reason, and the CLI exits 2. It does not retry.
     - Launch deadline: a `launch` that has no `launch_admitted` within `BUILDLOG_MEM_WAIT_LIMIT_S` (default 900 s; 0 refuses at the first hold) refuses with `launch wait limit reached without admission`. The limit runs on all three paths: a transport wait and a checker retry never extend it. An `admit` has no such limit.
