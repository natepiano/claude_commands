# Shutdown per session: each session settles, stops and returns on its own

> **Status: IMPLEMENTATION PLAN — phased, delegate-ready.** `/shutdown` keeps one record per session. Each session stops as soon as it is ready and, when a restart was asked for, comes back at once; restarts launch in parallel and are confirmed by the session registry; one session can be taken out, forced or restarted while others are still settling.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Restarts launch in parallel and are confirmed by the session registry  · status: done

#### As-built

- `_restart_record` runs a real restart in three steps. (1) Every non-seat entry except the requester's is launched through `_launch_entry_safely`: hosts other than `ghostty`, `zed` and `terminal` go to a `ThreadPoolExecutor(max_workers=RESTART_LAUNCH_WORKERS)` with `RESTART_LAUNCH_WORKERS = 6`; those three window hosts launch one after another on the calling thread, because desktop switching needs `WINDOW_DELAY_SECONDS` (1.2 s) between windows. (2) `_confirm_entries` makes one shared wait for every launched id, then calls `confirm_entry` on each. (3) The requester's entry is launched and confirmed alone, last. `_process_seats` and `_finish_record` follow. `_entry_order` keeps only requester-last and name order, so a showrunner launches without any unit live.
- `launch_entry(record: ShutdownRecord, entry: ShutdownSessionEntry, restarted_at: str, desktop_state: DesktopLaunchState, *, dry_run: bool) -> EntryLaunched | EntrySettled` does everything up to and including `_launch_session` and the failed-launch liveness check. `EntryLaunched(relaunching_pending_timers: bool)`: a process was started, or found live after its launcher failed, and the registry must confirm it. `EntrySettled`: nothing is left to confirm (already complete, live and noted, manual, failed, liveness unknown, or any dry run).
- `confirm_entry(record: ShutdownRecord, entry: ShutdownSessionEntry, restarted_at: str, live: bool) -> None`: not live sets `restart failed{session not live after 90 seconds}`; a live showrunner gets its restart note; timers are restored and the entry becomes `restarted` or `timers pending`.
- `wait_for_sessions(session_ids: frozenset[str], timeout: float) -> frozenset[str]` polls `_live_session_ids()` every `SESSION_POLL_SECONDS` (0.5) until every id is live or `timeout` has passed, and returns the ids live at the last poll. `_confirm_entries` passes `SESSION_WAIT_SECONDS` (90.0).
- `_launch_entry_safely` turns any exception from one entry's launch into `restart failed` with the error's reason on that entry and returns `EntrySettled`; the record then ends `restart partial` through `_finish_record`.
- `MachineRestartResult.elapsed_seconds` holds the wall time of `_restart_record`; a real restart ends its result lines with `<machine>: restarted <n> in <s> s` (`_timing_line`). A dry run stays serial, in `_dry_run_entry_order` (units, showrunners, top-level, requester), and prints its former lines.
- `_tmux_launch`: when `tmux new-session` fails and a second `has-session` probe finds the tmux session, it opens a window there; when the session is still absent the original error is raised.
- `add_unit.py`: with a `UnitRestoreLaunch`, `main` records the launch, prints `<unit> restore launched: tmux attach -t <session>` and returns 0 without waiting for remote control (`wait_for_remote_control` is not called), whether it launched the session or found the marked unit already running. New-work launches keep the wait and `--timeout`.
- `sessions.running_session`: a registry record with a non-empty `procStart` is running only when it equals `process_start(pid)`, socket or not; a record without one falls back to the messaging socket.

**Files:**
- `scripts/shutdown/restart.py` — `launch_entry`, `confirm_entry`, `wait_for_sessions`, the three-step `_restart_record`, the timing line, the tmux recovery.
- `scripts/production/add_unit.py` — a restore returns once launched.
- `scripts/message/sessions.py` — `running_session` proves the process whenever a start is recorded.
- `scripts/shutdown/test_restart.py`, `scripts/production/test_add_unit.py`, `scripts/message/test_sessions.py` — the cases for the above; all derive from `ShutdownTestCase`.

**Binds later work:**
- `launch_entry`, `confirm_entry`, `_launch_entry_safely` and `_confirm_entries` take `ShutdownRecord` and `ShutdownSessionEntry` until the per-session shutdown records replace those types; `wait_for_sessions` takes only session ids and does not change with them.
- A dry run prints exactly its former lines in its former order, pinned by `test_restart_dry_run_fixture_preserves_output`. Only a real restart prints the timing line.
- One entry's launch failure never stops the others (`test_one_launcher_exception_leaves_other_entries_restarted`); a relaunch of a single session keeps that rule.
- Record updates from launch threads are serialized by the account lock: `_account_lock` in `scripts/shutdown/record.py`, a `flock` on a new descriptor per call.
- A restore through `add_unit.py` proves nothing about the session; its caller confirms through the registry.

**Gotchas:**
- Slow-restart diagnosis (restart of the claude 3 account, 2026-10-12): eight tmux-hosted sessions took 90 to 92 s each, the whole of `add_unit.py`'s 90 s `--timeout`; ten took 2 to 6 s. `wait_for_remote_control` polls `tmux capture-pane -p` every 0.5 s for `/remote-control is active`, and `capture-pane -p` reads only the visible screen. The panes are 80x24 on the alternate screen with history size 0, and the restart note is 13 to 17 rows tall. On the eight the active line was written 0.26 to 4.64 s before the note, so it was never on the visible screen at a poll; on the ten it was written 0.44 to 2.73 s after the note. Remote control itself was active on all eight within about 3 s: each transcript holds a `bridge_status` entry with the active line, and each registry record's `bridgeSessionId` equals the id in that line's URL. The wait failed on screen layout, not on remote control, so a relaunch is confirmed through the registry and never through pane text.
- `bridgeSessionId` in `~/.claude/sessions/<pid>.json` is the registry field that shows remote control. Its null case was read from the 2.1.296 binary, not observed on a session.
- `sessions.running_session` is stricter than the socket rule and reaches every caller of `running_sessions` and `live_sessions`: a reused pid beside a leftover socket is not running. `inventory.py` already required `procStart == process_start(pid)`, so shutdown's own listing gains no condition. The rule is not yet confirmed on macOS, where `process_start` costs one `ps -o lstart=` call per registry record per call; on Linux it reads `/proc/<pid>/stat`.
- Two entries launched together can share one tmux session that does not exist yet. Creation is not serialized; the launch that loses `new-session` recovers in `_tmux_launch` (`test_parallel_tmux_creation_recovers_by_opening_a_window`). Any rewrite of the launch pool keeps that recovery.

**Ruled out:**
- A lock around tmux session creation: a competing creator can be another process.
- Reading remote control from pane text, or typing into a pane.
- Reporting a session that is back without remote control from `confirm_entry`: no session was in that state.
- A timing line on a dry run: the dry run prints exactly its former lines.

Record-level issues are not carried. An old record that does not validate raises `InvalidRecord` naming the file.

### Phase 3 — Each session stops when ready and returns at once; one conductor per machine  · status: done

#### As-built

- **Store.** `scripts/shutdown/record.py` holds one `SessionShutdown` per session; the account record (`ShutdownRecord`, `ShutdownScope`, `Conductor`), the slot API, `park`/`unpark`/`parked` and `stop_lock` are deleted. `SessionShutdown` and `AccountHold` carry a required `request_id: str`, made once by `down` and given to the peer, so requester-last and hold clearing never compare `requested_at`. `SessionProgress` has `stopping{at}` and `restarting{at, prior}`; `launch-blocked` treats `stopping` as held by name.
- **`migrate(login) -> list[str]`** runs under the account lock, called by `begin`, `conduct`, `up`, `cancel` and `now`. It writes each old-format entry as a session record with `request_id` `legacy-<machine>-<requested_at>`, at most one per session id (a new-format file, else the slot record's entry, else the newest parked record's entry); writes a hold that `account_hold` infers from the old files as `hold.json` without replacing an explicit one; moves `record.json` and `partial/*.json` to `history/`; and returns one line per file, `shutdown: converted the <state> record of <label> from <time>`. An old `cancelled` or `up` record moves to `history/` and writes no session record; record-level issues are not carried. An old file that does not validate, or a conflicting session id, raises `InvalidRecord` naming it before any write or move. Until a writing verb migrates them, `session_records`, `find_session`, `account_hold` and `all_account_holds` read old files through the same private converter, so `status`, `records`, `launch-blocked`, `held-sessions` and a dry run see an old shutdown without changing a file; the old wire types and validators stay private to that converter and no writer uses them.
- **Request.** `settle.begin(login, requested_by, only, *, then, alerts, request_id) -> list[SessionShutdown]` migrates, then creates a `waiting` record, with its Codex seats, for each non-seat session under `only` that has none, and records and stops its timers (a timer stop failure takes that one session out again and names it). A session that has a record is left alone and listed as `already in progress`; with no `only` it sets `hold: "account"` and calls `set_account_hold`. A seat whose owner is not in the request gets no record, and `status` lists it from inventory. `settle.down(account, here, only, *, then, alerts)` keeps the `github-warm-status` check, runs `begin` and starts this machine's conductor, also when every named session already has a record (this resumes a shutdown whose conductor died); unless `--here`, it runs `down <login> --here --requested-by <id> --request-id <request id> [--only …] [--restart] [--no-alert]` on the peer. A peer that is unreachable or refuses prints `<machine>: not reached; run /shutdown there` and never undoes or delays the local shutdown; exit 0 when this machine started.

Window hosts launch one at a time through the shared desktop state, never through a pool. The conductor ends when no record on this machine has a step left: each is archived, or `stopped` with `then == "stop"`, or needs the user (`stop failed`, `restart failed`, `manual restart`, `timers pending`). It calls `clear_account_hold` when the last record carrying the hold's `request_id` is archived, and on ending sends the requester one result (`report_stop`'s text, key `shutdown-result-<login>`) built from this machine's records plus one read of the peer's `records --json --here`. Every `SETTLE_INTERVAL_SECONDS` it takes one inventory and calls `next_step(record, owner: FreshSession, others: list[SessionShutdown], now) -> Step` for each record, where `Step` is `SendSettleMessage | StopSession | RelaunchSession | Hold(reason: str) | Finished`:
  - `waiting`, settle message due, not the requester → `SendSettleMessage` (a showrunner is messaged at once). The requester's record gets `Hold("requester goes last")` until every other record of the same `request_id` on this machine is finished, failed or archived.
  - `ready` with no blocker and the session and each of its seats idle, or `force == "now"` → `StopSession`. `session absent` or `session identity lost` → `StopSession` too: the stop records `already gone` or `process identity lost`, signals nothing and cleans up. `session account unreadable` → `Hold("account unreadable")`.
  - `stopped`, `already gone` or `process identity lost` → `RelaunchSession` when `then == "restart"`, and `Finished` with the record kept for restore when `then == "stop"`. `restarted` → `Finished`: archived, never launched again.

The conductor and `stop_session` build `FreshSession` (`matching session` with `seats: list[Session]`, `session absent`, `session identity lost`, `session account unreadable`) with the one `_fresh_session`, which never writes an observed pid over the record's.

### Phase 4 — Acting on one session: take out, force, restart; what holds each one  · status: done

#### As-built

- `--only NAME[,NAME...]` is public on `down`, `cancel`, `now` and `restart`, and `restart --here` is public. Resolution never holds local work back for the peer: `--here` resolves on this machine only; a session seen in both records and inventory counts once; an id found on this machine resolves without the peer; when the peer is not reached a name resolves from this machine alone, stderr says `<peer>: not reached; names checked on <machine> only`, and an item found nowhere exits 2 naming the peer as not reached. - `cancel --only X` takes out a `waiting` or `ready` record (timers and footers back, queued shutdown messages retired, the session told to carry on, the record archived) and prints `<name>: taken out`. - `now --only X` sets `force: "now"` on that record alone, calls `_launch_conductor` and prints `<name>: stopping now`; an in-progress record, or a cleanly stopped one with no work left, prints `<name> is <progress>; nothing to stop` and exits 1. `now` stops nothing itself: the conductor owns every stop and every retry, so a `stop failed` record, or a stopped one with leftover cleanup (`record.stop_incomplete`), is retried by the conductor and its result reaches the requester through `report_stop`.
